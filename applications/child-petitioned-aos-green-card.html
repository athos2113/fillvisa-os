Help your parents make the United States home

Explore the Child-Petitioned AOS Green Card Application for eligible parents living inside the United States. A U.S. citizen son or daughter age 21 or older petitions for the parent, who applies for permanent residence through adjustment of status.

Get Started
  • Free form tools
  • No signup

FAMILY REUNIFICATION

Who is this case for?

Eligible parents who are physically present in the United States and qualify to adjust status through a U.S. citizen child age 21 or older.

Parents of adult U.S. citizens

Qualifying parents are immediate relatives. Eligible applicants may file I-130 and I-485 together without waiting for a preference-category visa number.

Individual applications for each parent

Each parent needs a separate I-130 and I-485. The parent’s spouse or other children cannot be included as derivative applicants. Permanent residents cannot petition for parents.

PREPARE YOUR FAMILY’S CASE

Forms Included in this Application

The child files the family petition and the parent files the adjustment application. Eligible immediate relatives may file them together, or reference an existing I-130 receipt or approval. Confirm adjustment eligibility before filing.

Core petition and adjustment forms

  • If not already filed

    I-130

    Petition for Alien Relative. The U.S. citizen child documents citizenship, age of at least 21, and the qualifying relationship. File separately for each parent.

    Start form
  • Required

    I-485

    Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status. The eligible parent applies for a green card from inside the United States.

    Start form
  • Unless exempt

    I-864

    Affidavit of Support. The petitioning child generally submits this even when a joint sponsor is needed. A qualifying joint sponsor submits a separate I-864, unless the applicant qualifies for an exemption.

    Start form
  • As required

    I-693

    Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record. Arrange the examination with a USCIS-designated civil surgeon and submit the required medical record with I-485.

    View medical instructions
  • Required

    Supporting evidence

    Gather the child’s birth and citizenship records, relationship evidence, the parent’s identity and admission or parole records, and any I-130 receipt or approval. Follow the immediate-relative checklist.

    Review USCIS instructions
  • If needed

    I-864A

    Contract Between Sponsor and Household Member. A qualifying household member may use this when contributing income or assets to the sponsorship.

    View USCIS form

Optional requests and filing support

  • Optional

    I-765

    Application for Employment Authorization. Eligible adjustment applicants may request a work permit while I-485 is pending; filing alone does not authorize work.

    Start form
  • Optional

    I-131

    Request advance parole, when eligible, for travel while adjustment is pending. Generally obtain it before departure; exceptions and travel risks depend on the case.

    View USCIS form
  • Payment option

    G-1450

    Authorize a credit card payment for an eligible paper USCIS filing. Confirm current fees and payment instructions for each form.

    Start form
  • Payment option

    G-1650

    Authorize an ACH transaction from an eligible U.S. bank account for a paper USCIS filing. Use the applicable payment method.

    Start form
  • Optional

    G-1145

    Request an email or text when an eligible USCIS lockbox filing is accepted. This does not replace formal receipt notices.

    Start form
Plan for financial sponsorship. The petitioning child generally must submit I-864 and meet U.S. domicile requirements. If income is insufficient, review qualifying assets, household-member contributions, or a joint sponsor. Follow the current I-485 instructions for any applicable exemption.

I-130A is for spouse petitions and is not required for a child’s petition for a parent. For a parent outside the United States, see the child-petitioned consular application, which uses DS-260 instead of I-485.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Basic Eligibility Requirements

Being in the United States and having a U.S. citizen child does not automatically establish adjustment eligibility.

Get Started

  • A qualifying family relationship

    The petitioning child must be a U.S. citizen age 21 or older. Document the parent-child relationship; step-parent and adoptive-parent relationships have additional requirements.

  • Presence and entry requirements

    The parent must be in the United States and generally have been inspected and admitted or paroled, unless a specific exception applies. Review the full immigration history and any adjustment bars.

  • Visa availability and status

    Immediate-relative visas are not subject to annual numerical limits. Certain status-violation and unauthorized-employment bars do not apply to immediate relatives, but this does not remove entry requirements, other bars, or admissibility requirements.

  • Admissibility and support

    Meet the applicable medical, financial-support, and other admissibility requirements, or qualify for an available exemption or waiver. An approved I-130 alone does not grant status or work authorization.

Official guidance: USCIS adjustment instructions, I-130 instructions, and immediate-relative adjustment guidance. Fillvisa provides form tools and general information, not legal advice.

ONE STEP AT A TIME

Getting Started

Confirm eligibility, collect the parent’s and child’s records, and prepare a separate application for each parent.

Explore the forms
  1. 1

    Confirm eligibility and timing

    Confirm the child’s citizenship and age of at least 21, the qualifying relationship, and the parent’s entry and immigration history. Determine whether to file concurrently or reference an existing petition.

  2. 2

    Collect documents and arrange the medical

    Gather relationship, identity, immigration, and financial evidence. Schedule the civil surgeon examination and follow current medical submission instructions.

  3. 3

    Review, file, and track the case

    Check current editions, fees, filing location, and signatures. Keep copies and respond to USCIS notices, including biometrics or interview appointments when required.
